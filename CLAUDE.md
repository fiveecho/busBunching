- `controlFunction.py` (19 Oct): `make_controller(T_c=3600, delta=0, r_min=None, r_max=None)` returns `control(bus, tracker, now) -> r`. Equivalent-arterial coordinate (`Z_STOP`, `equivalent_position`) and the spring law; see Controller design. `simulate_run(controller=..., control_period=10)` calls it every `control_period` ticks for moving buses, and also on each bus's departure tick (`time == math.ceil(bus.dwell_end)`; `dwell_end` is a float, so `==` alone never fires), and sets `bus.speed = bus.base_speed * r` (`base_speed` is the drawn leg speed, set at dispatch and at each stop, so r never compounds). Without the departure-tick call a bus drove 0-9 s uncontrolled after every stop. `control_period` is the driver display refresh interval (s): a sample-and-hold, so roughly a delay of one period; period 1 would cost about 10x more controller calls. `make_controller` is a closure: it binds T_c, delta, r_min, r_max so `sim.py` only ever calls `controller(bus, tracker, now)` (equivalent: `functools.partial` or a class with `__call__`). `control` returns 1.0 for a terminal bus (guard; the sim already skips them) and ends with `max(r, 0.0)`, a hard physical floor in every stage that `r_min` cannot bypass (buses never reverse; at T_c = 120 s stage 1 reached r = 0.09). `busBunchVisualisation.py` takes a controller (currently `None`) and animates every run, bunched or not.
- Driver lag (1 Nov): `simulate_run(..., driver_lag=0)`. `Bus.r_cmd` is the displayed command (set on refresh ticks: every `control_period` and on the departure tick); `Bus.r` is the applied fraction, moved each tick by (1 - exp(-1/driver_lag)) (r_cmd - r) (exact first-order lag for 1 s ticks); speed = base_speed * r every moving tick. r does not evolve while dwelling. Verified: with driver_lag = 0, seeded runs are identical to the previous code (controlled and uncontrolled). Ethan chose a stored r_cmd (smooth per-tick lag) over lag-on-refresh-only.
- `routePull.py`: refreshes the route cache.

Tick-based, 1 s ticks. Speeds are km/h in the constants and converted to km/s at use. One run takes about 0.066 s (measured), so 5000 runs is about 5 min on one core.

- **Dispatch:** a bus every H seconds from t = 0 while t <= 7200 (25 buses). No dispatch jitter (decision: perfectly even dispatch is an assumption, probably optimistic).
- **Speed:** one draw per leg, when the bus leaves a stop. Truncated normal (SciPy), defined in `helpers.get_speed(expressway, traffic_factor)`. Arterial mean 24.5 km/h, sd 5 x factor, bounds mean ± 3 sd with floor 5 and cap 50. Expressway mean 43, sd 1 x factor (was 3 until 22 Oct), bounds mean ± 3 sd with floor 3 and cap 60. Factor 0 returns the mean directly (before any division by sd). Symmetric ± 3 sd bounds keep the mean exact (24.53 at factor 1.4, where the floor starts to bite) and the actual sd within 2% of nominal. History (18 Oct): fixed bounds [15, 45] pushed the mean up with noise (trip time fell above factor 1.0, a real effect, 4x the CI); ± 1 sd bounds kept the mean but cut the actual sd to 0.54 sd, so factor labels were wrong. Do not use either. Means calibrated to the timetable (see Calibration).
- **Dwell** at every intermediate stop (indices 1-29): 15.6 s fixed (5.6 s door overhead plus about 10 s acceleration/deceleration loss) plus 1.52 s x Poisson(rate x wait). rate = 10/300 pax/s, the same at every stop (no passenger-volume shape). `wait` is the time since the previous bus *departed* that stop, clamped at 0. The first bus uses wait = H (ghost predecessor). `last_bus_time` is set to arrival plus dwell. Dwell is a float (no longer truncated with `int`). Mean dwell is 29.3 s, not 30.8: because `wait` runs from the previous departure, a bus sees about H - d, so d = 15.6 + 1.52 (300 - d)/30 gives d = 29.3 s (about 9 boardings, 13.7 s boarding). Only the first bus gets 30.8 s. Simulated: mean 29.3 s, sd 5.8 s per stop.
- **Tick quantisation:** on arrival the position is clamped to the stop and the overshoot is discarded, and dwell starts at that tick, so up to about 1 s is lost per stop (about 1% of trip time). Small, but it biases calibration slightly.
- **Bunching:** consecutive dispatch indices, follower position >= leader position, both non-terminal. Both indices go into a set. The run continues after the first bunch. Buses are not blocked from overtaking (assumption).
- **Metric per run:** len(set) / n_buses. Reported: mean and std (ddof=1, in percent) over runs, plus the share of runs with at least one bunch (99+% at the current noise level, so uninformative).

## Controller design (to implement)

Base: Daganzo and Pilachowski (2011), "Reducing bunching with bus-to-bus cooperation", Transportation Research Part B 45(1), 267-277. Speed control from front and rear spacings, as if buses were joined by springs.

Verified from the PDF (Ethan supplied it, 23 Oct): control law eq. (7); alpha* = 0.63 lambda b vbar (15); delta* = 5.0 r sqrt(lambda b vbar) (16); var(s) = r^2/(2 alpha) (12) and 0.79 r^2/(lambda b vbar) at the optimum (17); condition alpha Dt in (0, 0.5); worked example alpha* 0.38/h, delta* 1.3 km/h, spacing sd 0.40 km; Table 1 ranges (lambda 10-100 pax/km/h, R_T^2 0, 0.1, 0.4, vbar 25-60, Dt 5 or 20 s, S/D 2, 4, 8); clipped law in 6.1 is middle{0, vbar + C_n, vbar}, "easy ... in simulation but hard to analyze", and lowering delta to 0 raises speed but also headway variance. Their model is a ring; they argue closedness does not matter with many buses. Combined noise rate R^2 = R_T^2 + lambda gamma S B^2 c'^2 (traffic plus passenger). Connections to this project:
- Sampled stability edge: their update (11) has eigenvalues 1 - 2 alpha Dt (1 - cos theta) (eq. 25), i.e. z = 1 - K T. Fastest mode needs alpha Dt < 0.5, i.e. T_c > 2 Dt: the 20 s edge at a 10 s refresh is their condition (Ethan reproduced it for this setup, using the paper) (they also need it for non-negative coefficients in their variance proof).
- The lower gain edge exists only because variant A omits their first term: substituting it cancels the lambda terms (eq. 8), so their law is stable for any alpha > 0. Their boarding model (4), c'_n = v'_n (1 - lambda b s_n), linearises to beta = lambda b vbar. The stability window is their analysis applied to a variant without the cancellation term; it shows what that term buys. Present it as applying their results, not extending them.
- Stage 3 tests their proposed implementation: section 6.3 suggests a screen showing each bus's current "speed limit" that drivers obey approximately, and says Dt barely matters so drivers need not monitor continuously. Footnote 5 notes the commanded cruise speed (which includes traffic disturbances) must be related to the bus's top speed in traffic (the r_max reasoning). Use this as the README framing for stage 3.
- Method 1 (eq. 18) adds only the fixed stop delay tau vbar per stop, since their first term handles boarding; Ethan's equivalent-arterial coordinate adds the full mean dwell (boarding included), consistent with omitting that term.
- Appendix B: forward-looking-only control is 20% worse in speed penalty and 25% worse in spacing variance than two-way.

- Paper law: v'_n = vbar + C_n, with C_n = [lambda b vbar (s_n - S) + alpha (s_n - s_rear) - delta] / (1 - lambda b s_n). The first term cancels the passenger-loading feedback, the second is the spring term, and delta is headroom so the command rarely exceeds vbar. Equilibrium speed is c - delta.
- Fixed stop delay tau: Method 1 uses effective spacings s* = s + tau (z_ahead - z_n) vbar. Method 2 uses true spacings with C_n scaled by (1 + tau vbar / D)^2 and works about as well when buses are at least about 2 stops apart. Ethan's buses are roughly 3-4 stops apart, so Method 2 is probably enough. The (1 + tau vbar / D)^2 factor is eq. (20) in the paper (verified 23 Oct from the PDF).
- Notation: lambda means both the passenger rate (pax/km/h) and the Laplacian eigenvalue, and tau means both the fixed stop delay and the driver lag. Use distinct symbols in code and README (for example q for demand, tau_d for driver lag). "c" in "equilibrium speed c - delta" is the commercial speed c-bar = vbar (1 - lambda b S) (paper eq. 2), cruise speed reduced by boarding, not vbar (verified 23 Oct).

## Analysis (predict, then compare with the simulation)

- Each variant reduces to a graph Laplacian with eigenvalue lambda(theta) = sum_j c_j (2 - 2 cos(j theta)) + k. Gershgorin bounds the neighbours-only case to [0, 4]. A ring has theta = 2 pi m / N; an open chain with free ends has lambda_k = 2 - 2 cos(k pi / N).
- Per mode, the loop is 1/(s + alpha lambda e^{-sT}/(tau s + 1)). Stability for any lag/delay reduces to a Nyquist/gain-margin check at lambda_max. Without lag, the pole is -alpha lambda. For a pure delay T the condition is alpha lambda_max T < pi/2. With T = 10 s the critical alpha is about 141/h versus about 1.2/h in use, so lag and delay are not binding.
- Sampled-time effects are negligible: alpha*dt about 3e-4 at 1 s ticks.
- Steady-state spacing variance is sigma^2/(2 alpha) (paper eq. 12), from the white-noise (H2) response. Finite-time variance per mode: Var_k(t) = [2(1 - cos theta) sigma^2 / (2 alpha lambda)] (1 - e^{-2 alpha lambda t}) + e^{-2 alpha lambda t} Var_k(0). After 1 h the spacing variance reaches about 87-96% of steady state (ring, N = 8-12, alpha 1.0-1.6/h).
- Paper design formulas: alpha* about 0.63 lambda b vbar; delta about 5 sigma sqrt(lambda b vbar); var(s) about 0.79 sigma^2/(lambda b vbar). Needs 0 < alpha*dt < 0.5. Tested only for lambda 10-100 pax/km/h; Ethan's route is about 156 (120 pax/h per stop x 29 stops / 22.3 km), so use with caution.
- Ethan's derivation of the open-chain spectrum (23 Oct; worked through by him following Lestas and Vinnicombe section III and Daganzo and Pilachowski, with Claude's help. Attribute it that way: a known result reproduced for this setting, not original work): (1) Gershgorin: interior rows centre 2 radius 2, end rows centre 1 radius 1, L symmetric, so lambda in [0, 4]. (2) Interior recurrence -y_{n-1} + (2 - lambda) y_n - y_{n+1} = 0, characteristic r^2 - (2 - lambda) r + 1 = 0, discriminant lambda(lambda - 4) < 0 on (0, 4): complex roots with product 1, so r = e^{+-i phi} on the unit circle and lambda = 2 - 2 cos phi. Repeated roots: lambda = 0 (r = 1) gives the constant mode (genuine); lambda = 4 (r = -1, y = (A + Bn)(-1)^n) cannot meet the boundary conditions, so it is not an eigenvalue of the open chain. (3) Ghost buses as mirror conditions, 1-indexed: y_0 = y_1 gives B = A tan(phi/2), so y_n ~ cos((n - 1/2) phi); y_{N+1} = y_N gives -2 sin(N phi) sin(phi/2) = 0, so phi = k pi / N. Result: lambda_k = 2 - 2 cos(k pi / N), mode shapes cos((n - 1/2) k pi / N), k = 0..N-1. Ring analogue: substitute e^{i n theta}, theta = 2 pi m / N (the corner -1 entries are the wrap-around neighbour; e^{i N theta} = 1 makes the wave join up). Modes: k = 0 all buses equally late (lambda 0, uncontrolled by the spring); highest k alternates +- along the chain (fastest, the one that zig-zags at short T_c).
- Boarding model for the analysis (23 Oct). Not a stochastic delay: two parts. (a) Mean part = plant feedback: extra front-headway error e_n gives about b r e_n extra dwell per stop (b r = 1.52/30, about 0.05); with a stop every T_s (about 95 s on arterial legs), lateness grows as d l_n/dt = beta e_n = beta (l_n - l_{n-1}), beta = b r / T_s, about 1.6-1.9 /h (same as q b vbar; value depends on averaging over the stop-free expressway). One-sided coupling: matrix beta B, B = +1 diagonal, -1 subdiagonal (not symmetric). (b) Random part = disturbance: Poisson count sd about 3 pax, about 4.6 s per stop, independent; additive noise like traffic noise. Full linear model in lateness l: dl/dt = (beta B - alpha L) l + noise. Ring: B -> 1 - e^{-i theta}, L -> 2 - 2 cos theta, pole s(theta) = (beta - 2 alpha)(1 - cos theta) + i beta sin theta. Real part: stable iff alpha > beta / 2 (the 74 min edge). Imaginary part: disturbances travel along the platoon as waves. Daganzo's omitted first term is -beta e_n, which cancels B. Sampled version (10 s display refresh): per-mode pole z = 1 - K T with K = (2 alpha - beta)(1 - cos theta) (real part only); stable iff 0 < K T < 2 for all modes, giving one window 2T < T_c < 2/beta, about 20 s to 74 min. Continuous analysis is the K T << 1 limit (s = ln(1 - K T)/T ~ -K); at T_c = 10 min, K T is about 0.07, so the continuous model is accurate there. Simulation check of the 20 s edge (23 Oct, stage 1): share of updates where r reverses direction is 85% at 15 s (sd of r 0.76), 74% at 20 s, 49% at 30 s, 24% at 2 min (random noise would give about 67%), matching the predicted negative real z-pole (alternating) for T_c < 40 s and marginal at 20 s. Oscillation clipped at r >= 0 raises mean r (stage 1 trips shorten to 56.5 min at 15 s); stage 1 EWT goes slightly negative there because EWT subtracts the scheduled H/2 and buses arrive more often than scheduled (a limit of the EWT definition). Stage 2 trip-time rise at short T_c is mostly large slow-down commands (floor binds 11% at 30 s), with oscillation adding below about 25 s.
- Coupled open-chain check (23 Oct, numerical): eigenvalues of beta B - alpha L with ghost ends (front bus: no boarding feedback, B[0,0] = 0; L ends = 1), beta = 1.61/h. Stability edge alpha > 0.807/h (N = 8, 12), 0.808 (N = 20), against ring 0.806 and formula beta/2 = 0.805: the ring result holds for the open route. Disturbances do not affect stability (linear system); they set fluctuation size (the cut H2 work). Caveat: B is not symmetric, so the matrix is non-normal; disturbances can grow transiently as they travel along the chain even when all eigenvalues are stable (related to string stability). One README sentence, no extra work. Recheck (23 Oct, same conventions, zero mode excluded, brentq): edge 0.805/h for N = 8, 12, 20, i.e. exactly beta/2; the earlier 0.807/0.808 probably came from a looser root-find or slightly different end conventions. Modal approximation (Ethan's question, 23 Oct): project B onto L's eigenvectors v_k and keep only the diagonal. The diagonal is exactly lambda_k/2: B's symmetric part is L/2 except end terms diag(-1/2, 0, ..., 0, +1/2), and v_k^T D v_k = (v_k(N)^2 - v_k(1)^2)/2 = 0 because the cosine modes are symmetric or antisymmetric about the centre. The dropped skew part (the travelling-wave term) couples modes; its entries are not small (up to 0.77) but leave the edge unchanged (0.805 both ways), and at alpha = 6/h (T_c 10 min) the approximate eigenvalues are within about 1% of the exact ones (which are all real there). Per-mode open-chain loop: alpha lambda_k e^{-sT/2} / (tau s + 1) / (s - beta lambda_k / 2), the open-chain version of the ring's real part; at lambda_max the plant pole is about 2 beta (lambda_max < 4). The symmetric-part (energy, V = |l|^2) bound stays positive even at alpha = 6/h (+0.10/h), so |l| can grow instantaneously from some initial patterns: concrete evidence of non-normal transient growth at the chosen gain. Justification for using L's eigenvectors (Ethan's "single derivative vs Laplacian" framing, 24 Oct, verified numerically): B = L/2 + C + D exactly, with C = centred first difference (l_{n+1} - l_{n-1})/2 (skew-symmetric: advection, the travelling-wave part) and D = diag(-1/2, 0, ..., 0, +1/2) (end terms). So beta B - alpha L = -(alpha - beta/2) L + beta C + beta D: boarding is advection plus anti-diffusion of strength beta/2, the spring is diffusion alpha, and the edge is net diffusion alpha - beta/2 > 0. Half of B IS the Laplacian, which is why L's eigenvectors work; only C and D are approximated. On a ring D = 0 and V = |l|^2 gives a rigorous Lyapunov proof (l^T C l = 0) with no eigenvectors; on the open chain the rear-bus +beta/2 term breaks that (hence the positive symmetric-part bound). Local (one bus, neighbours as inputs) view plus row Gershgorin on beta B - alpha L: all discs in Re <= 0 iff alpha >= beta (T_c <= 37 min), a rigorous, diagonalisation-free sufficient condition, conservative by 2x against the exact beta/2; T_c = 10 min (alpha = 6/h) satisfies it with large margin. A single bus with neighbours treated as exogenous inputs (true MISO) is NOT a valid stability test on its own: it breaks the feedback through the neighbours; Gershgorin is what makes the local view rigorous.
- Open route versus loop: information spreads only about sqrt(2 alpha t), roughly 1.5 buses per hour, so ring results are a fair approximation for interior buses (end spacings about 10% higher). Dispatch irregularity left after one 52 min trip (N = 12, ring/open): jitter about 3%/3%, independent random headways 13%/18%, slowly varying (lowest mode) 57%/87%.
- Lestas and Vinnicombe (2007), "Scalability in heterogeneous vehicle platoons", ACC 2007, FrA14.4, pp. 4678-4683: VERIFIED from the paper (Ethan supplied the PDF, 23 Oct). Setup: open chain of N vehicles following an independently moving leader x0 (NOT a ring), spacing errors E_i, tridiagonal L (eq. 5; one end tied to the leader, other free). Section III (homogeneous): L symmetric, L = U Lambda U^T, so the platoon splits into N SISO loops 1/(1 + lambda P(s)) (Lemma 1); Gershgorin gives lambda in [0, 4] (same steps as Ethan's derivation). Remark 1: with no leader information (K_l = 0), lambda_min -> 0 as N grows and the double-integrator P(jw) has large gain and phase near -pi at low frequency, so lambda P approaches -1 and disturbance response is not uniformly bounded in N. Reference implementation (tested; H2 work cut from scope 22 Oct, kept for reference):

```python
import numpy as np
trap = getattr(np, "trapezoid", None) or np.trapz   # NumPy 2 renamed trapz

def lam(theta, c=(1.0,), k=0.0):
    return sum(cj*(2 - 2*np.cos((j+1)*theta)) for j, cj in enumerate(c)) + k

def spacing_var(N, alpha, sigma2, c=(1.0,), k=0.0, tau=0.0, T=0.0):
    """H2 spacing variance on a ring of N buses. Time in hours."""
    w = np.logspace(-4, 4, 200001); s = 1j*w; tot = 0.0
    for m in range(1, N):
        th = 2*np.pi*m/N
        Z = 1/(s + alpha*lam(th, c, k)*np.exp(-s*T)/(tau*s + 1))
        tot += (2 - 2*np.cos(th))*sigma2/np.pi*(trap(abs(Z)**2, w) + 1/w[-1])
    return tot/N
```

## Metrics

- Primary: percentage of buses bunched per run (set of buses involved; a lone pair counts as 2). State this definition in the README. Report the mean with 95% CI (1.96 x std/sqrt(runs)). No paired seeds (see Decisions). Use Wilson intervals for proportions.
- EWT per checkpoint stop (indices 10, 20, 29), using arrival times sorted in physical order: h = diff(sorted arrivals); AWT = sum(h^2)/(2 sum(h)); SWT = H/2; EWT = AWT - SWT. Leave out the first and last bus (by dispatch index, before sorting). When the mean headway equals H, EWT = (H/2) CV^2 (population sd), so EWT 1 min corresponds to CV about 0.63 and 2 min to about 0.9 at H = 5 min. With ddof=1 and mean headway drifting from H, the identity holds only to a few seconds; use it as a check, not an equality.
- Implementation (`headway_stats`): `np.array(arrival_times[1:-1])` gives (buses, stops); `np.sort(A, axis=0)` sorts each stop's column independently (plain `np.sort` sorts each bus's row, which is already sorted, so it fails silently); `np.diff(..., axis=0)` gives headways. Sorting handles overtaking: passengers board whichever bus arrives next. Ragged lists (a cut-off bus) make `np.array` raise, which is acceptable as a loud failure. Sanity check: EWT at stop 0 is exactly 0 (even dispatch). Ignore column 30 (terminal, alighting only).
- EWT is quadratic in headway, not mean |H - h|: a gap h collects passengers in proportion to h, each waiting h/2. Gaps (200, 400) give EWT 16.7 s; (100, 500) give 66.7 s (4x), while mean |H - h| only doubles.
- SWT is H/2, not the scheduled arrival time. EWT ignores a shift common to all buses (all buses 5 min late gives EWT 0). In Laplacian terms this is the lambda = 0 mode, which Daganzo's spring law (A) also does not control; variant B's reference term does. Worth one README sentence: A should improve EWT without improving schedule adherence. Schedule deviation (actual minus noise-free calibrated times) is an optional extra metric.
- `BusStop.last_bus_time` is the previous bus's departure, so `time - last_bus_time` is the boarding window (about H minus dwell), not the headway. Do not use it for EWT.
- Also record headway sd (s) and mean trip time (the cost of control). Headway sd, not CV, is what Phase 3 compares with the H2 prediction (design numbers above are given as headway sd). CV = sd/mean headway is redundant with EWT.
- Route-average EWT: mean over stops 1-29 (skip 0, which is always 0, and 30, the terminal). With uniform demand this equals the per-passenger average excess wait; it must become passenger-weighted if a demand profile is added. A median over stops is not useful (EWT rises steadily along the route; there are no outliers). Over runs, use the mean (EWT is right-skewed; bad days count), optionally with the 90th percentile.

## Decisions already made

Python (not C++). Shared congestion component skipped (so independent per-bus noise makes the baseline pessimistic; list it as a limitation). Dispatch jitter dropped. No full paper; a README. Metric is the percentage of buses bunched per peak window rather than time to first bunch. No per-run seeds or paired comparison (14 Oct): offset noise with run count instead. Run-to-run sd of % bunched is about 12-15 pp (12.3 at baseline, 18 Oct), so the 95% CI on a difference between two settings is about ±5 pp at 50 runs, ±2.5 at 200 and ±1.1 at 1000; use about 1000 runs where differences are small (delta sweep).
